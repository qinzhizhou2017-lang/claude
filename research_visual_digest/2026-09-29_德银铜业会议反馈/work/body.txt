Industry Update
     
DB Copper Conference: Feedback & Key 
Themes
Last week, we hosted our annual Virtual Copper CEO Conference, featuring senior 
executives from many of the world's largest copper producers, alongside leading 
industry experts. Within this report, we summarise the key discussion points and 
conclusions from each session. Replays are available for a limited period via the 
conference platform (registration link).
Key industry themes – illustrated by our favourite quotes
n The market is facing an acute supply squeeze: “We are in an environment 
for the foreseeable future of high and probably very volatile copper pricing... 
There is a very genuine risk of seeing the first stock-out in China in all our 
careers... Social inventory cover in China is exceptionally low... Any price 
dip is seeing aggressive buying". Many investor questions focused on 
downside risks to copper prices, suggesting a degree of scepticism around 
the sustainability of recent price strength.
n Under-supply is structural and will take years to solve:  “The entire 
ecosystem [for building large-scale projects] in the West has atrophied, 
creating major challenges in adding supply… The biggest constraints to 
adding supply fundamentally sit above ground rather than below ground… 
The equivalent of 12 mines the size of Cobre Panama need to come on line 
over the next 6-7 years in order to meet demand".
n Demand is resilient and underpinned by electrification: "For every $2bn of 
spend on hyperscalers, you need about 150-170 kt of copper… For each 
megawatt of capacity in a data center, you need between 30-50 kt of 
copper… there will be substitution, but it won't be disruptive to the market". 
Despite elevated copper prices and ongoing macroeconomic uncertainty, 
companies broadly reported no material signs of demand weakness across 
key markets.
n Companies are looking to grow and increase copper exposure: "We have 
a huge commitment to grow in copper… We're working on ways to unlock 
new copper supply from new technologies [in order to help fill the supply-
gap]…We have incredible organic growth options in copper". While 
companies are clearly preparing to increase investment in growth projects, 
permitting and labour were consistently highlighted as key constraints on 
future supply growth.

Record prices: driven by fundamentals or the tariff threat?
Figure 1: LME copper price at record levels
Source : Deutsche Bank, Bloomberg Finance LP
The potential introduction of a US tariff on refined copper has been a dominant 
theme across the copper market over the past 18 months, driving inventory 
movements, reshaping trade flows, and contributing to much tighter physical 
market conditions and higher prices. The US has accumulated around 1.6 mt of 
copper inventory since the start of 2025 (including ~0.7 mt held on exchange), but 
this material remains effectively unavailable to the ex-US market. Outside the US, 
inventories are ~300 kt, equivalent to only 2.5 days of consumption, leaving China 
at risk of a potential stock-out if current trends persist.

The attending management teams broadly expect prices to remain high and 
volatile. Most believe that the threat of US tariffs and related inventory movements 
have inflated prices, but also noted that prices have a strong underpin from tight 
supply-demand fundamentals . The concentrate market is viewed as 
“exceptionally tight”, the industry’s project pipeline remains limited and strong 
demand growth is expected from a number of sectors, including grids, renewables, 
energy storage, and AI/data centres. Most believe that sustained higher prices will 
be required to incentivise new supply.

Despite prices closing in on $15,000/t, demand has remained resilient and supply 
continues to underperform. According to our Expert Panel, available inventories are 
very low, with social inventory cover in China exceptionally low, and physical 
buying emerges quickly on even small pullbacks. The Panel believes near-term 
price risks remain skewed to the upside. Physical indicators including LME and 
SHFE backwardation and rising Chinese premiums point to increasing scarcity. If 
US inflows continue, the panel sees scope for copper prices to move even higher, 
particularly given exceptionally low ex-US inventory cover.

The persistent weakness in global mine supply remains a key area of fundamental 
support for the copper market, and has been a major driver of price strength over 
the past 12 months. Years of underinvestment, declining ore grades, and 
disruptions at several major operations have constrained supply growth, with 
recent company updates and high-frequency data suggesting global mine supply 
could decline in 2026 following growth of less than 1% in 2025. Most participants 
at the conference see downside risk to industry based production guidance of a 
5-6% increase in YoY mine supply in 2027, with disruption-adjusted concentrate 
growth potentially closer to 2-3% according to our Expert Panel. Concentrate 
tightness looks increasingly structural according to corporates. Smelting 
capacity continues to exceed concentrate availability, driving spot treatment 
charges to record negative levels.

years of strong growth, refined output is expected to flatline or modestly decline

Is the copper investment cycle about to takeoff?
Following years of underinvestment, a significant copper investment cycle is 
drawing closer as the industry seeks to develop new sources of supply. A key debate 
with investors is whether permitting challenges and capital discipline will constrain 
investment relative to the previous major investment cycle or if the sector is at risk 
of overinvesting. On the project front, the attending companies continue to view 
lengthy and complex permitting as a key challenge, although a shortage of skilled 
labour is emerging as the biggest bottleneck on adding new supply. While there 
are a large number of projects globally, these vary considerably in scale and 
readiness, with permitting, water and the industry's ability to develop multiple 
projects simultaneously influencing how quickly new supply can reach the market.
Higher incentive prices are needed to unlock new supply. Long-run consensus 
copper prices have already increased ~20-25% over the past 18 months, but rising 
capital costs and competition for project-development resources could require 
further increases. The panel suggested that the market has become increasingly 
comfortable with long-term copper price assumptions above ~$12,000/t.
Demand outlook and risks
On the demand front, corporates did not highlight any major signs of weakness 
from the recent surge in prices and see a strong structural demand outlook, 
supported by electrification, renewable energy investments and accelerating AI/
data-centre investment. Demand appears increasingly price-inelastic in key 
growth areas, including grid investment, US policy-driven demand (i.e. inventory 
building) and AI-related infrastructure. Ongoing thrifting and substitution towards 
aluminium is expected at higher prices, although these are not seen as major 
threats and not expected to materially alter the medium-term outlook. Key 
downside risks are seen as a US policy reversal (removing the tariff threat) and 
macro weakness.
Industry consolidation and M&A
Discussion on and interest in M&A was considerably lower compared to last year, 
largely due to several large deals announced or attempted in 2025 (less so in 2026). 
We expect industry consolidation to remain a key trend as producers seek to secure 
future copper growth in an increasingly constrained supply environment. 
Corporates did not give much away on M&A strategies, highlighting an 
opportunistic, value-based approach, and also stressing the strength of organic 
opportunities. The Anglo Teck merger is expected to complete within the next six 
months, we believe merger talks between RIO and GLEN could be revised at some 
stage, and strategic mergers between existing copper companies is also a 
possibility, in our view.

Expert Copper Market Panel
The Deutsche Bank Copper Expert Panel included Colin Hamilton (Market 
Research and Economic Analysis, Teck Resources), Nicholas Snowdon (Head of 
Metals Research, Mercuria).
n The copper market remains very tight. Despite prices closing in on $15,000/
t, demand has remained resilient and supply continues to underperform. 
Available inventories are very low, with social inventory cover in China 
exceptionally low, and physical buying emerges quickly on even small 
pullbacks.
n Regional inventory dislocation is central to current market tightness. The 
US has accumulated around 1.6 mt of copper inventory since the start of 
2025 (including ~0.7 mt held on exchange), but this material remains 
effectively unavailable to the ex-US market. The US remains the most 
attractive destination for swing units and the panel expects inflows to 
continue into year-end while tariff uncertainty persists. Outside the US, 
inventories are ~300 kt, equivalent to only 2.5 days of consumption, leaving 
China at risk of a potential stock-out if current trends persist.
n Near-term price risks remain skewed to the upside.  Physical indicators 
including LME and SHFE backwardation and rising Chinese premiums 
point to increasing scarcity. If US inflows continue, the panel sees scope for 
copper prices to move further higher, particularly given exceptionally low 
ex-US inventory cover.
n Mine supply remains the key structural constraint. Global mine 
production was close to flat in 2025 and a similar outcome is expected in 
2026, with recent weather-related disruptions adding further pressure. The 
panel sees downside risk to industry based production guidance of a 5-6% 
increase in mine supply in 2027, with disruption-adjusted concentrate 
growth potentially closer to ~2-2.5.
n Concentrate tightness looks increasingly structural. Smelting capacity 
continues to exceed concentrate availability, driving spot treatment 
charges to record negative levels. With limited new concentrate production 
on the horizon, the panel sees little prospect of the market escaping this 
deficit without sustained mine supply growth.
n Chinese refined production is now constrained by raw-material 
availability (scrap and concentrate) following strong growth in H2’25 and 
early 2026. After several years of strong growth, refined output is expected 
to flatline or modestly decline through Q4 and into early 2027 as 
concentrate and scrap availability tighten. This removes what had 
previously been one of the more bearish elements of the copper balance.
n Scrap is providing less of a release valve than historically. Chinese policy 
changes have reduced scrap-market liquidity, while several years of 
destocking has also limited underlying availability. Policy adjustments 
could improve scrap flows in 2027, but the panel cautioned against 
expecting this to materially loosen the market given already-low 
inventories.
n Demand appears increasingly price-inelastic in key growth areas. Grid 
investment, US policy-driven demand and AI-related infrastructure were 
highlighted as areas where buyers may be less sensitive to copper prices 
than traditional consumers. This could mean higher prices are required to 
balance the market, although some copper thrifting and substitution are 
already taking place.

n Project execution and capital efficiency have improved materially, with

Lundin Mining: Jack Lundin, Chief Executive Officer
n Vicuña Stage 1 remains on track for a potential sanctioning decision by 
year-end, with RIGI fiscal stability and provincial agreements secured and 
early site preparation well underway. The company’s current focus is on 
completing the detailed engineering and budgeting for Stage 1, with a final 
investment decision expected later this year. Stage 1 capex is likely to 
increase from the current $7.1bn (real) estimate, reflecting inflationary 
pressures and fully incorporating project risks and contingencies. We 
would not be surprised to see Stage 1 capex increase to over $8bn.
n The company remains confident in Vicuña's execution strategy, 
highlighting the depth of technical work completed at Josemaria, a strong 
ownership team (50:50 JV with BHP), and integration of the mine plan with 
engineering and independent technical reviews. Recent severe weather in 
Chile is also feeding directly into Vicuña's design, including greater 
consideration of backup power, winter access and operating contingency.
n In terms of funding Vicuña, the company has various options available. The 
company recently increased its revolving credit facility (‘RCF’) to $4.5bn 
and is of the view that it could fund its 50% share of Vicuña through its RCF 
and internally generated cash flow. However, LUN and BHP are evaluating 
alternative financing structures including project finance and streaming, 
which could potentially be introduced further down the line during the 
construction phase.
n Looking ahead to Stages 2 and 3, further information is expected to be 
released to the market during 2027 as the near-term focus remains firmly on 
sanctioning Stage 1. Longer term, the development could utilise LUN’s 
existing Chilean infrastructure, including port and expandable desalination 
capacity, with potential cross-border water and concentrate infrastructure. 
Discussions with Chilean and Argentine authorities around extending the 
existing binational framework into the exploitation phase are progressing.
n LUN recently reduced its 2026 production guidance to 300-325 kt (from 
310-335 kt) after a second severe winter storm disrupted recovery efforts at 
Caserones and delayed the planned return to normal operations. 
Operations have since returned to steady state and the company is 
confident that it can meet its revised guidance, although Q3 is expected to 
show a significant impact.
n LUN continues to progress its low-capex intensity brownfield growth 
across its existing operations, targeting 30-40ktpa of incremental copper 
production over the next 2-4 years. The Saúva deposit at Chapada is the 
most advanced opportunity, followed by a potential ~10 ktpa cathode 
expansion at Caserones, as well as underground expansion at Candelaria.
